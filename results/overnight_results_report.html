Dynamic-alpha: a continuous fusion weight instead of two fixed buckets

Null

results/dynamic_alpha_ablation.csv · results/dynamic_alpha_bootstrap_significance.csv

New tonight, inspired by DAT (Hsu & Tzeng, 2025, arXiv:2503.23013) — but adapted, not ported: DAT drives its continuous weight from raw BM25-vs-dense score dominance, and a direct check on this corpus found that signal inverted here (entity-heavy queries score lower on raw BM25, mean 17.8 vs. 33.0 for open-ended — a query-length confound, not a match-quality signal). The adaptation instead counts independent structural entity signals (course code / alias / faculty initial / faculty name) and interpolates λ continuously between 0.5 and 0.9.

Recall/MRR come out byte-identical because the exact-match score ceiling already guarantees top-1 for unambiguous entity queries regardless of λ — it only affects ranking below the ceiling. And on this 200-query test set, entity_signal_strength never exceeded 0.25 (99 queries at 0.0, 101 at 0.25) — no query names two-or-more independent entity types at once, so the "continuous" mechanism rarely got to act any differently from a near-binary split. A real, reportable null, not a failure to hide.

Baseline's dominant failure under this stricter lens isn't just silence — it's conflating different people's info into one false answer 42% of the time ("merged"). Both fixes nearly halve that (42%→24%). flat_notice and conditioning landed on identical aggregate distributions — checked this wasn't a scoring bug: 53/55 queries got the same per-query label, and the other 2 flipped in opposite directions and happened to cancel out exactly.
Read "complete" with its scoping caveat: it sits near-zero (0–1.8%) in every condition. AmbigDocs' own task is enumerating every valid interpretation in one answer; this system is deliberately designed to ask a short clarifying question and let the conversation continue, not front-load every candidate's full details. A near-zero "complete" rate likely reflects that task-shape mismatch as much as it reflects a real gap — not "the system fails at disambiguation 98% of the time."
